1. Overview

PromoAccess reads the weekly grocery flyers of Quebec banners and makes them readable with a screen reader.

  • Download the flyers of the banners you follow.
  • Search every flyer at once for a product.
  • Keep standing favorites, re-checked at every sync.
  • Build a shopping list and export it to a file.

Everything is kept on your computer. Once a sync is done, browsing, searching and list building work without a connection.

The whole application is reachable from the keyboard. Tab moves forward, Shift+Tab moves back, Escape closes a window. The five tabs are Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+5, and the tab name is announced before the control the focus lands on.

2. First steps

Three things to do once, in this order.

Your postal code

Press Ctrl+1. The focus lands on the postal code field. Type the six characters in one block, with no space: J3P7S7. Upper or lower case, it does not matter. Press Enter, or F5, to validate.

Then press Tab once: a read-only field names the town found for that code. It is how you check you typed it right.

The banners you follow

Still in Ctrl+1, Tab down to the banner list. Space ticks and unticks. Ticking downloads that banner's flyers right away; unticking removes them from the cache. There is nothing else to confirm.

Sync

F5, at any time, downloads the flyers of every followed banner. Once a week is enough, ideally Wednesday or Thursday when the new flyers come out.

After that, Ctrl+2 shows the flyers and Ctrl+3 searches them.

3. Settings — Ctrl+1

The focus lands on the postal code field.

Postal code
Six characters in one block. Enter or F5 validates and syncs.
Location
Read-only, one Tab away. Names the town for the code you typed.
Banners to follow
Space ticks and unticks, and it takes effect immediately. Only followed banners appear in the flyers, the search and the favorites.
Week
This week, next week, or both. Banners publish next week's flyer before the current one expires, so both weeks are often in the cache at once. This filter applies everywhere.
Hide items with no price
Some flyer entries are section headings or advertising panels with no product behind them. Ticked, they are left out.
Language
French or English. The whole interface switches. Product names come from the banners and stay as they wrote them.

4. Flyers — Ctrl+2

The focus lands on the flyer list. The tab order is: flyer list, Sort by, items of the chosen flyer, item details, then the buttons.

Flyer list

Banner, title, number of items, valid until. A banner often publishes several flyers at the same time: the main book, smaller inserts, and sometimes a separate prepared-meals book. That is what the title column is for. Choosing a flyer fills the item list below.

Items

Item, price, banner, valid until.

  • Enter adds the selected item to the shopping list.
  • Ctrl+D speaks the details of the selected item.
  • Ctrl+Shift+O opens the product page in your browser, when the banner provides one.

The buttons on this tab: Add to list, Add to favorites, Details, Product page.

5. Search — Ctrl+3

The focus lands on the search field. Ctrl+F gets there from anywhere. The search runs on the cache, never on the network: results are instant and work offline.

How the words are matched

  • Every word you type must appear somewhere in the item.
  • Accents are ignored: cereales finds CEREALES.
  • Case is ignored.
  • A fragment counts: fromag finds fromage.

Filters

Banner
One banner, or all of them.
Week
This week, next week, both.
Maximum price
Leave it empty for any price. Items advertised without a number, such as “2 for $5”, are kept: they can easily beat the ceiling.

Enter in the search field, or the Search button, runs the search. The number of results is announced and the focus moves to the first one.

6. Favorites — Ctrl+4

The focus lands on the Banner dropdown: the store is chosen first, then Tab leads to the items found, already narrowed to it. The favorites themselves are one Shift+Tab back, where the rules are edited rather than the deals read.

A favorite is a rule, not a pinned product. Flyer items get brand new identifiers every week, so a favorite pointing at one would be dead in seven days. What is stored is the words to look for, re-checked at every sync.

The tab order is: favorites, Sort by, Banner, On sale right now, item details, New, Edit, Delete.

Creating one

The New button, or Add to favorites from any item in Ctrl+2 or Ctrl+3. Enter on a favorite edits it. Delete removes it, with a confirmation.

In the favorite window

Words to look for
All of them must appear.
Banners
A checkbox list. Tick as many as you want; none ticked means every banner.
Maximum price
Empty for any price.
Whole words only
Ticked, “ail” finds garlic and not chicken wings. Unticked, a fragment is found anywhere, so “fromag” finds “fromage”. Ticked by default.
Active
Untick to pause a favorite without deleting it.

The banner

A Banner dropdown sits just before the items found. Pick a store, then Tab: the list shows only its items. "All banners" brings everything back. Only stores that actually have something appear, and the choice is remembered between sessions.

On sale right now

Everything the active favorites matched this week, without duplicates. Enter adds an item to the shopping list. Original prices are fetched for these without being asked for.

7. Shopping list — Ctrl+5

The focus lands on the Banner dropdown: the store first, then Tab for the list. Columns: item, quantity, price, banner, valid until.

Adding

Enter on any item in Ctrl+2, Ctrl+3 or Ctrl+4, or the Add to list button.

A small window then asks for the quantity, with 1 already filled in: type a number if you want several, or just press Enter. Escape cancels and adds nothing.

Adding the same item twice does not make two lines: the quantity of the existing line goes up, and the application announces the new total. Note that "the same item" means the same name, the same banner AND the same price — a banner sometimes advertises one product twice in a week at two prices, and those are two different decisions, so two lines.

Working on the list

Quantity
A button, which opens a small window with a number.
Remove
The Delete key, or the button. No confirmation: it is one line, and adding it back is one keystroke.
Empty the list
A button, with a confirmation.

Total

Below the list, a read-only field Tab reaches. It gives the number of items and the estimated total — two tins of the same thing are two items, on one line. Estimated, because an item advertised as “2 for $5” carries no unit price and counts as zero.

When a line holds several units, its price column shows what the line is worth and the arithmetic behind it: “$13.98 (2 x $6.99)”. The total can then be checked line by line.

The banner

As in the favorites, a Banner dropdown comes before the list. Pick a store and Tab: you see only what to buy there, and the total follows — "IGA: 6 items, estimated total 29.83 $". That is what will be spent in that store, not the whole week's shopping.

Export — Ctrl+S

Writes the list to a file. Three formats are offered: plain text, CSV for a spreadsheet, and Markdown.

In text, PromoAccess writes one file per banner: "list - IGA.txt", "list - Maxi.txt" and so on, beside the name you gave. One file per store is one file per trip. The export always holds the whole list, whatever banner is showing on screen. CSV and Markdown stay a single file: a spreadsheet wants one table.

8. Sorting the items

A Sort by dropdown sits just above each item list, on Ctrl+2, Ctrl+3 and Ctrl+4. One setting for the three, remembered between sessions.

Price, lowest first
The default. Items advertised without a number go last, rather than pretending to be the best deal in the flyer.
Name, A to Z
Accents are ignored, so an item beginning with an accented E sorts with the E words and not after Z.
Discount, highest first
Only about half the items carry a percentage: the banners do not all send one. Those are not treated as zero — they go together at the end.
Brand
Same story. Roughly half the items name a brand, and it varies wildly from one banner to the next.

After each change the application announces how many items actually carry the value you sorted on and how many do not — “2 with a percentage, 29 without”. The count is spoken after the name of the sort, so you hear what you chose and then what it gives.

9. Details of an item

The details field

One Tab past each item list, on all three tabs. Read-only, and it never wraps: one Down press is one fact, so you can walk through it line by line.

It holds the name in both languages when the banner gives both, the price, the original price and the saving when they are known, the format, the sale story, the banner, the dates, and the address of the product page. Entering the field asks the banner about that item at once.

Ctrl+D

Speaks the same thing as one sentence, without moving the focus. Useful while arrowing down a list.

Why two items can look identical but for the price

Banners advertise the same product several times in one week: a different format, a different variety, a members-only price, or the same deal repeated in two of their books. The feed does not always say which. When it says nothing, neither can we — the details field shows what was actually sent, and nothing invented.

Ctrl+Shift+O

Opens the product page on the banner's own site. Advertising redirectors are stripped first, so what opens is the product and not a click counter. When the address cannot be cleaned, nothing is opened.

11. What the banners provide

PromoAccess shows what the banners send, and nothing else. It never guesses a price, a format or a saving. When something is missing here, it was missing in what the banner published.

What is often missing

  • The original price. Only about one item in five carries one, which is why the saving is not always available.
  • The brand and the discount percentage. Roughly half the items, and it varies enormously: one banner sends percentages and no brands, another sends brands and almost no percentages.
  • The product page. Some banners provide none at all.

There are no departments

No banner classifies its products by aisle — meat, bakery, produce. The flyer pages are named “Page 1”, “Page 2”, and an item carries no section at all. The grouping you would see on paper exists only in the printed image.

Some flyers are English only

Rare, but real: a few banners publish no French version. Costco's general merchandise book is one of them. The interface stays in your language; the product names cannot.

Two weeks at once

A flyer for next week appears before the current one expires. That is normal. The Week filter decides what you see.

12. About

Where your data lives

Everything is in a single file, cache.db, in the PromoAccess folder of your Windows user profile: flyers, favorites, shopping list and settings. Exported lists land there too.

Nothing is sent anywhere. The only connections made go to the flyer service, when you sync or ask for the details of an item.
